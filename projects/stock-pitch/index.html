andrew kim / projects / stock pitch model

a monte carlo valuation model i built for the jec invesco stock pitch competition. it simulates 100,000 five-year cash-flow paths for a company and gives back a whole distribution of fair prices, so the pitch could talk about a range and the odds instead of one number.

placed in the top three. invesco's judges called the model the strongest part of the pitch.

three scenarios

each run is a discounted cash-flow valuation, but every input that i'd normally have to guess is drawn from a distribution instead. those are revenue growth, free-cash-flow margin, the discount rate, and the terminal growth rate. the model runs three versions of the world, a base case, an inflation shock that raises the discount rate and squeezes margins, and a demand drop that cuts the first two years of growth, so the pitch could show how the company holds up when things go wrong.

it's all vectorized with numpy, and it uses a fixed seed, so the same inputs give the same chart every time. there's also a faster screening version that runs a lighter valuation over every s&p 500 tech and communication stock and ranks them by upside.
